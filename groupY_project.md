## Key Figures  F.
The development of the internet was built upon numerous engineers, scientists and organisations over the decades. Each played an important role in developing the technologies and systems that helped transform early computer networks into the global internet we use today.
1. J.C.R Licklider (1915-1990)
Joseph Carl Robnett Licklider was an american psychologist and computer scientists who played an important role in developing the early ideas of the internet. In 1962, Licklider introduced his idea of the "Galactic Network", where he suggested the idea of developing a place where computers in different locations could be connected, allowing people to access information and communicate with each other from long distances. He also worked at the Advanced Research Projects agency (ARPA), where he encouraged other researchers to develop computer networking technology. His idea helped inspire the devlopment of ARPANET, Which becae an important foundation for the modern internet.

2. Vint Cerf (1943)
Vint Cerf is an American computer scientist who is widely known as one of the fathers of the internet. Cerf worked closely with another well known computer engineer Robert khan during the 1970s to develop the TCP/IP (Transmission Control Protocol/Internet Protocol). They designed a system that allowed different computer networks to exchange data. TCP/IP was adapted as the standard communication protocol for ARPANET during the 1980s. This helped making the foundation for the global internet.

3. Robert Elliot Kahn (1938)
Robert Kahn is an American computer engineer and scientist who also played a major role in developing the technology that allows different networks to be able to communicate with each other. Kahn worked on ARPANET and found the idea that different computer networks needed a common system to exchange information. As previously mentioned earlier, he worked with Vint Cerf in the development of the TCP/IP.

4. Tim Berners-Lee (1955)
Tim Berners-Lee is a Brittish computer scientist who is well known for inventing the World Wide Web. He worked at CERN, the European Organisation for Nuclear Research, in Switzerland. In 1983, he proposed the idea of a way for scientists to share information through linked documents. He devloped three important technolgoies.
HTML (Hypertext Markup Language): The language used to create web pages
- HTTP (Hypertext Transfer Protocol): The system that allows web browsers and servers to communicate
- URL (Uniform Resource Locator): The address used to locate information and websites online
Berners-Lee also created the first ever web browser and web server in 1991.

5.Leonard Kleinrock (1934)
Leonard Kleinrock is an American Computer scientist who contributed to the technology that's allows computers to communicate over networks. Kleinrock did research into packet switching which is a method of sending information by breaking it into smaller pieces called packets. These packets then travel across a network and are put back together when they reach their final destination. In 1969, Kleinrock's research team at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA), helped send the first message over ARPANET to another computer at Stanford Research Institute. The team sent the word "LOGIN", but unfortunately the system crashed and only transmitted the two letters "LO".

## First Viewpoint Analysis

### Engineering viewpoint  
Many new innovations have been produced throughout the progress of the Internet from ARPANET to World Wide Web. In 1969, the Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) developed ARPANET, which led on from this to eventually become our current Internet. An innovative ‘packet’ switching mechanism was incorporated into ARPANET which could segment data and then transmit it. Data packets were directed along the network by Interface Message Processors (IMPs) which would hold received packets and then pass them onto the subsequent node. ARPA wanted to produce a robust and distributed network which would also be able to resist a nuclear attack. 

Throughout the 1970s further networks were developed. These included ALOHAnet which used radio waves to send packets of data using ultra high frequency and was based in Hawaii. Additionally, the first Local Area Networks (LANs) utilising Ethernet were developed. This type of network, which was commercially released in 1980, is used locally and can also be used on a Metropolitan Area Network (MAN) scale. Ethernet has progressively superseded most other wired networks. It has adapted so that it can now transmit data at increased bit rates, is able to support increased numbers of network nodes and can also span larger geographical areas. Despite this advancement, Ethernet still enjoys high levels of backward compatibility. Ethernet is recognised as pivotal to the Internet. It is extensively used within both homes and industry and can successfully be combined with Wi-Fi. Indeed, the ALOHA protocol was even used within Ethernet networks. Following Ethernet, and after regulation changes during the early 1980s, ALOHA like random access approaches could also be applied to Wi-Fi and mobile telephony networks. However, there was a requirement for these, and indeed other contemporary, networks to interact with each other. Unfortunately, this proved impossible as they adhered to completely separate network models. Consequently, there was an idea to devise a novel ‘internetworking’ network model that could link dissimilar networks together, without necessitating any changes to the original network models. This issue was addressed in the 1970s when Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn, both engineers, developed two key protocols (TCP and IP) that are part of the Internet protocol suite. Their aim was to segregate out data directing and data dependable transmission. The Internet Protocol (IP) is a network layer protocol and is used for network interlinking - thus effectively establishing the basis of the Internet. IP is responsible for transmitting data packets from one host to another. It does so purely by analysing the IP address details given within each packet header (Wikipedia 2026). The Transmission Control Protocol (TCP) guarantees that data in the form of a byte sequence is accurately, sequentially and with verification, transmitted between applications which run on hosts connected by an IP network. Consequently, TCP/IP is often used generically to refer to these protocols as TCP operates in conjunction with Internet Protocol (IP). These protocols have provided a very innovative answer to this issue. They provide an adaptable and open network model which allows for interaction between a variety of differing networks. Even though TCP/IP did provide a method for addressing the above issue, the Internet continued to remain a restricted environment. It was only accessible by experts as there was no overarching system in place. 

Tim Berners- Lee suggested the World Wide Web to CERN in 1989. This is a worldwide information sharing system which utilises the Internet. The Web is essentially a network of hyperlinks. In order to optimally share information, Berners-Lee incorporated 3 key components within web servers. Firstly, Uniform Resource Locators (URLs) are text strings which identify specific documents. Secondly, Hypertext Transfer Protocol (HTTP) is the protocol used to handle client-server requests. Finally, documents are organised using Hypertext Markup Language (HTML) and hyperlinks can also be embedded. Moreover, the Web was successfully merged with existing systems such as TCP/IP and the Domain Naming System and hypertext. As a result, the Internet developed into a widely accessible and easy to use global information sharing facility. Since 1993, the general public has had access to this.

## Societal viewpoint

The Internet has completely changed human connection and culture. Instead of only communicating locally, people now interact instantly within online communities across the globe. This has transformed how culture is shared and expressed. This shift has significant downsides. As the internet developed, more people got caught up in digital spaces to the point where they ignore the people who are physically around them. This can lead to isolation. The internet has also played an important role during the COVID-19 pandemic, which is also what boosted the rate at which the internet was used for communication, education, and work because of the need for social distance. This also created a reliance as an unwanted consequence, that being a severe overdependence on technology. Finding a balance between the benefits of digital connectivity and the risk of losing real world interaction is now a new challenge that came with the internet in modern society (Li et al., 2022).

Another major shift in society caused by the internet is how culture created and shared. Before the internet cultural trends were mostly dictated by local communities or large media companies. Today platforms like youtube tiktok etc, allow anybody to share their art and ideas music globally. While this has made culture more accessible it has also created online communities where only people with the exact same views socialise
